Infalsus Controller Wiring

Six buttons go on the left column; six LEDs (driven by 2N2222s) and the slider go on the right. The three-digit numbers printed on the board (009, 111 …) are nRF pin numbers. Any nice!nano v2 or nRF52840 Pro Micro clone with the same pinout works.

Things to watch

Transistor lead order

Same TO-92 shape, different lead order from part to part. Read the marking on the body and match it to the table in the detail above. If unsure, use a multimeter in diode mode: red lead on one leg — if both other legs read 0.6–0.7V, that leg is B.

Swapping C and E does no damage, but the LED will be very dim or off. If you see that, rotate the transistor 180°.

The two resistors do different jobs

220Ω sets the LED current ((3.3V − 2V) ÷ 220 ≈ 5mA). 4.7kΩ limits the GPIO-to-base current to 0.55mA and protects the nRF pin. Neither can be omitted and they cannot be merged.

LED current comes from the 3.3V regulator, so all six lit at once (30mA) is fine. The GPIOs together only source 3.3mA.

LED polarity

Long leg (+, anode) toward the 220Ω, short leg (−, cathode) toward the transistor C. Backwards it just stays off — no damage.

Brightness is adjustable in firmware via PWM; the 220Ω sets the maximum.

The two capacitors

100nF between the slider wiper (029) and GND, close to the board, so it also filters noise picked up by the wiring. No polarity.

10µF between VCC and GND on the board that carries the LEDs and slider. It absorbs the 3.3V dip when LEDs switch on. For an electrolytic, long leg (+) to VCC, white stripe to GND.

Buttons (micro switches)

No polarity. Terminal COM to the pin, NO to GND. On a 3-terminal (SPDT) switch leave NC open — wiring NC instead reads as pressed when idle. The pull-up is inside the chip, so no external resistor.

If the terminals are unmarked, use continuity mode: the pair that beeps at rest is COM–NC, the pair that beeps when pressed is COM–NO.

RST switch

One small tactile switch between RST and GND. A single press reboots; a quick double-tap enters bootloader mode (a NICENANO drive appears) so you can flash firmware. Place it where it can be reached from outside the case.

Perfboard too small? Split off the LED circuit

Important. The six LED driver cells (transistors and resistors) do not have to live on the main board. If 4×6cm is getting tight, solder just the six LED cells onto a separate small perfboard (e.g. 3×7cm or an offcut) and fix it in empty space inside the case with double-sided tape or hot glue.

Only 8 wires need to cross between the boards: the six GPIOs (009 · 010 · 111 · 113 · 115 · 002) plus 3.3V and GND. Put the 10µF on the LED board.

Identifying 4-pole jack terminals

Which terminal is T/R1/R2/S varies by product. Insert a 3-pole cable, then use continuity mode to match each terminal to the tip, ring and sleeve on the far end of the plug. Two terminals will beep with the sleeve: the one bonded to the metal body is S, the other is R2.

PJ-320A breakouts usually have T/R1/R2/S or 1–4 printed on the silkscreen.

Slider

The two end pins go to VCC (3.3V) and GND, the wiper to 029. If the direction is backwards, swap the ends or flip MOUSE_INVERT_X in code. Use VCC, not RAW, or the ADC range will be off.
